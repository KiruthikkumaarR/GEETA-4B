Privacy notice — version 2, 13 October 2026. Written to follow the Australian Privacy Principles. [Operator: add your name and contact details below before inviting families.]

Who runs GEETA

GEETA is run by [name / organisation], contact [email / phone]. We collect only what's needed to help children practise maths and to show their grown-ups how they're going.

What we keep

  • Grown-up accounts: name, email, and a password stored only as a one-way scrambled "hash" — nobody can read it. If you turn on 2-step sign-in we keep the key your authenticator app uses.
  • Each child: a first name or nickname, year level, a picture and world theme, and a 4-number secret (also stored only as a hash).
  • A short learning summary after each session: which maths skills were practised, how many questions were solved, how many hints were needed, the kinds of slips made (for example "used the wrong jump size"), time spent, and the parent report (PDF).
  • A security log for grown-up accounts: sign-ins, wrong passwords and account changes, with the time and internet address they came from — kept for 180 days to spot break-in attempts. It never contains passwords, codes or chats.

What we never keep

  • The chat between your child and GEETA — it lives in the computer's memory during a session and is then thrown away.
  • Photos or drawings of homework — they are read once and deleted within seconds.
  • Anything from the camera. The optional focus helper runs entirely inside your browser and only ever sends "focused" or "looking away".
  • Phone numbers or addresses a child types — GEETA reminds them to keep those private and removes them from memory.

Why we keep it

  • To show parents and tutors what a child practised and how they're going (reports and the Progress page).
  • So GEETA can pitch its hints at the right level and remind a child about slips they made before.
  • To keep accounts safe (the security log).
  • If you joined our research study and agreed: de-identified results (a study code, never a name) may be used in a written report.

We never sell it, share it for advertising, or use it to train other companies' AI.

Where it lives and who can see it

  • Everything is stored on the GEETA computer itself (Adelaide, South Australia), not on a cloud service. The maths AI runs on that computer too.
  • When you use GEETA over the internet, the connection is encrypted (HTTPS) and passes through Cloudflare, a US company whose network carries the traffic to the GEETA computer. Cloudflare does not keep your child's learning data. You can ask to use GEETA on the home network only.
  • Daily emails: each evening a parent gets an email with a PDF of their child's day (only on days GEETA was used). It is sent from GEETA's email account (Gmail, run by Google) to the parent's email address, so a copy sits in both mailboxes. Parents can switch these emails off on the Parent page. Admins get a technical email with numbers only — no children's names, answers or chats.
  • A parent sees only their own children. A tutor sees only the children linked to them. The GEETA administrator can see all accounts to run the service.
  • Children can only use the maths page — they cannot see reports or other children.

How long we keep it

  • Learning summaries and reports: until you delete them, or [30 days] after you stop using GEETA.
  • Security log: 180 days. Backups: the 30 most recent nightly copies.

Your choices and rights

  • Delete your child's profile, learning history and all reports at any time from the Parent page ("Delete child's data").
  • Ask us what we hold about you or your child, ask us to correct it, or ask us to close your account: [contact].
  • Complaints: contact us first; if you're not satisfied you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

If something goes wrong

If we ever find that personal information may have been seen by someone who shouldn't, we'll act quickly to contain it and tell affected families — and the Information Commissioner where the law requires (Notifiable Data Breaches scheme).
