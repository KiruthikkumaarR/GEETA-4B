# GEETA maths study — information for parents and guardians

**Version 1 — 13 October 2026. DRAFT: for HREC review. Do not give to families until the study is approved.**
HREC reference: [number] · Investigator: [name, contact phone/email]

## What is this study?
We have made a maths "buddy" app called GEETA for children in Years 1–3. Your child shows GEETA a
homework question and GEETA helps them work it out **themselves** with small hints — it never just
gives the answer. We want to learn whether it is easy, safe and enjoyable for families to use over
4–6 weeks. Only two children are taking part, so this is a small "try it out" study.

## What would my child do?
- Use GEETA 2–3 times a week for about 20 minutes, with you nearby, for 4–6 weeks.
- Do a short 10-minute maths check (12 questions) at the start and again at the end.
- One of the two children uses GEETA; the other keeps doing homework as usual and is offered GEETA
  afterwards.

## What does GEETA keep?
| GEETA keeps | GEETA never keeps |
|---|---|
| Your name, email and password (scrambled so no one can read it) | What your child types or says to GEETA |
| Your child's first name or nickname, year level, chosen picture and secret number (scrambled) | Photos or drawings of homework (deleted within seconds) |
| After each session: which maths skills were practised, how many questions were solved, how many hints were needed, kinds of slips (e.g. "used the wrong jump size"), time spent, and your parent report | Anything from the camera |
| A record of sign-ins to grown-up accounts (time and internet address) for 180 days, to spot break-in attempts | Phone numbers or addresses your child might type |

Everything is stored on one computer looked after by the investigator, not on a commercial cloud.
If you use GEETA away from home, the connection is encrypted and passes through Cloudflare's network
(Cloudflare does not keep your child's learning data). Results we write up use a study code, never
your child's name.

## Is it safe?
- GEETA's maths is checked by computer code before your child sees it. In testing on 13 October
  2026 it made no maths mistakes and never gave away an answer.
- GEETA is kind when children feel frustrated, politely says no to non-maths questions, and reminds
  children to keep personal details private.
- Your child signs in with a picture and secret number only on a device you have switched on. They
  cannot see reports or anyone else.

## Possible risks
Your child might feel frustrated by a tricky question (they can stop at any time), or might share
personal details (GEETA asks them not to and does not keep them). As with any computer system, there
is a small risk of unauthorised access; we have tested and protected it carefully (see the full
application).

## Your choices
- Taking part is completely voluntary. You or your child can stop at any time, without giving a
  reason and without any consequence.
- You can delete your child's profile and everything kept about them at any time from your Parent
  page ("Delete child's data"), or ask the investigator to do it.
- Questions or worries: [investigator contact]. Complaints about how the study is run: [HREC
  contact — name, phone, email], quoting reference [number]. Privacy complaints may also go to the
  Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (oaic.gov.au).

# Child assent script (read aloud by the parent or investigator, first session)

> "This is GEETA. It's a computer helper that's really good at maths puzzles. It won't tell you the
> answers — it gives you little clues so YOU can work them out.
>
> We'd like you to try GEETA a few times a week for a little while, and do a short maths quiz at the
> start and the end. It's not a test for school and it doesn't matter if you get things wrong.
>
> You can stop any time you want, and that's totally okay — just tell us. You don't have to do it if
> you don't want to.
>
> GEETA won't remember what you type, but it will remember which kinds of maths you practised, so it
> can help you better next time. Please don't tell GEETA things like where you live or your phone
> number.
>
> Would you like to try GEETA?"
